# Super Metroid and its item randomizer: orientation

Super Metroid (SNES, 1994) is set on the planet Zebes. The randomizer these notes cover is the Super Metroid Item Randomizer served at samus.link, with its Casual and Tournament logics. Other randomizers (VARIA, DASH, and the SMZ3 combo with its Normal and Hard logic) use different rules; do not mix their facts into these notes.

## The story in brief

Samus Aran is a bounty hunter raised on the planet Zebes by the Chozo, a bird-like ancient race. The previous game ended with her sparing the last Metroid, a larva that hatched and imprinted on her as its mother. In this game she delivers that larva to the Ceres space colony for study, then leaves; Ridley and the Space Pirates storm in, kill the scientists and steal it. Samus escapes Ceres as it self-destructs and follows Ridley to Zebes, where the Pirates have rebuilt their base. She fights through the planet, kills Kraid, Phantoon, Draygon and Ridley, and enters Tourian, where the Pirates have bred more Metroids and revived Mother Brain. The grown baby Metroid ambushes Samus but relents; later it cripples Mother Brain, heals Samus, and is killed by Mother Brain. Dying, it gives Samus the Hyper Beam, which she uses to finish Mother Brain. Zebes self-destructs and Samus flies away; the ending varies with clear time, and any animals she saved are seen escaping.

Zebes has seven areas, each with its own notes: Crateria (the surface, which connects to every other area), Brinstar, Upper Norfair, Lower Norfair, the Wrecked Ship, Maridia and Tourian. The soundtrack is by Kenji Yamamoto and Minako Hamano; credited titles include "Brinstar: Red Soil Wetland Area" and two Maridia underwater tracks.

## How it is played

**In logic** means every item can be reached using only the listed items and tricks, with nothing more demanded of you. A location is in logic when both its area's entry rule and its own rule hold for the items collected so far. The generator guarantees all 100 item locations (84 tank and ammo locations plus 16 unique items) can be reached by logic alone. There are no starting items, and the goal is to defeat Mother Brain. The rules check access to rooms and items, not the fight itself.

**Placement.** Major/Minor split (the default) keeps majors (suits, beams, boots, tanks) in the 34 major locations and ammo in the 66 minor ones. Full randomization lets any item land in any of the 100 locations, so a spot that is an ammo pack in vanilla can hold a suit.

**Casual** logic is the cautious ruleset: it asks for the intended item at each obstacle. **Tournament** logic (the default) is the race ruleset: it accepts known cheaper routes such as hell runs, suitless Maridia and Spring Ball jumps. In every rule compared, anything Casual allows Tournament also allows.

Beams stack unless noted; Spazer and Plasma cannot be used together (all other combinations can). Item spheres are the orbs a Chozo statue holds; shooting one reveals the item. Visible items sit in the open and hidden ones sit behind blocks you shoot or bomb.

## Community vocabulary

Items go by short names: Morph, Bombs, Ice, Wave, Spazer, Plasma, Charge, Varia, Gravity, Hi-Jump or HJ, Space Jump or SJ, Speed, Screw, Spring Ball, Grapple, X-Ray, Supers, PBs, E-tanks, Reserves. The **Golden Four** or **G4** are the four boss statues. **The moat** is the water-filled stretch in Crateria between the gunship and the Wrecked Ship; several techniques cross it. **Red Tower** is the vertical shaft that leads into Red Brinstar; "climb the Red Tower early" is a common route. **The bridge** is probably the Early Super bridge fix in the randomizer ROM patch. **Early Supers** is the room reached by mockball. **Moondance** is the Etecoons access trick. Other room names: Mount Everest, Colosseum, Pants Room, Alcatraz.

## Routing arguments

Whether to climb the Red Tower early for Power Bombs before going to Norfair. Varia first versus a hell run. Gravity via the Wrecked Ship versus suitless Maridia (Tournament logic allows both). In speedrunning: skip Golden Torizo unless you need his items, save Crocomire until after Plasma, and Murder Beam versus charged Ice, Wave and Plasma on Mother Brain. In Major/Minor seeds, how much ammo to detour for. Boss order for the Golden Four is a matter of seed and taste; there is no consensus.

## Ten fun facts

1. Super Metroid released in Japan on March 19, 1994, North America on April 18, and PAL on July 28, on a 24-megabit cartridge.
2. Yoshio Sakamoto directed it at Nintendo R&D1, with programming by Intelligent Systems, under Gunpei Yokoi.
3. The opening narration is by Dan Owsen, a Nintendo of America employee.
4. It helped define the "Metroidvania" genre, alongside Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (1997).
5. The best ending needs a finish under 3 hours; 3 to 10 hours gets a middle ending; 10 or more is the lowest.
6. If you rescue the Etecoons and Dachora, they appear in the ending as a dot of light escaping the exploding planet.
7. Max health is 1499 energy with all 14 Energy Tanks (14 x 100 plus 99), and 400 more in Reserves.
8. Mother Brain has three forms: 3000, 18,000 and 36,000 HP, and the last needs 36 Hyper Beam shots at 1000 each.
9. The corner jump is also called the "flatley jump" (after Michael Flatley), and you can die at Ceres before the station explodes.
10. Draygon's corpse, if it drifts out of bounds on the left, loops back after about 18 minutes and 10 seconds.
