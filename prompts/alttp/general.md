# A Link to the Past and its randomizer: orientation

The game has two overlapping worlds. The Light World is the starting overworld (about 118 regions modeled). The Dark World is a corrupted parallel map (about 101 regions modeled) reached through portals, the pyramid or the Magic Mirror. Without the Moon Pearl in the Dark World, Link becomes a bunny who cannot use items, swing a sword or attack, and can only walk and pick up some free-standing items. A superbunny is a bunny that got inside an interior by cancelling with the mirror and can then open chests, swing a sword and dash there, even dying to revive as Link in a dungeon; the logic counts that as a sequence break. In Inverted mode the roles of the worlds swap.

In the randomizer, items and dungeon prizes (pendants and crystals) are shuffled, and optionally entrances, bosses and keys. A fact marked (vanilla) holds only in the unrandomized game.

Requirements for bosses, enemies and locations are what the logic accepts, which matches what really works in the game. The tracker calls a location unavailable, possible (reachable only if keys are spent well), out of logic (reachable only by a sequence break) or available.

World states: Open starts in the Light World, Standard starts with the guided Hyrule Castle intro, Inverted starts in the Dark World with the Moon Pearl rules flipped. Entrance randomization changes which door leads where, so vanilla placements no longer hold.

Sequence breaks the logic can allow: dark room navigation, bomb jumps, hovering with Boots, fake flippers, water walk, mirror superbunny, Somaria ice break, herapot, mimic clip, Moldorm bounce and several clip tricks.
